Pizza Retail, Analysis Companion
Read this next to the Pizza Retail Business Analysis dashboard. April 2026.

How to use

  1. Open Pizza Retail Business Analysis in Power BI Desktop. The .pbip is in the download row below.
  2. Read pages 1 through 5 in order. Each one asks a question the next one picks up.
  3. The dashboard is what you look at. This companion is what I wish someone had handed me alongside it: the question behind each page, the DAX that powers it, and the things it can't tell you.

Overview

Five pages. One story.

  • P1 Executive. Where we are, and whether anything is moving.
  • P2 Demand. When the money actually lands on the till.
  • P3 Menu. Which pizzas are pulling their weight.
  • P4 Behaviour. What the basket looks like once somebody orders.
  • P5 Action. What I'd change on Monday.

Important caveat up front. The data is transactional only. There are no customer IDs, no costs, no store locations, no staff rosters. Every conclusion on every page is topline, and every recommendation is a revenue bet rather than a margin bet. Keep that in the back of your head.

P1Executive Summary

Question

How's the business doing, and is anything moving fast enough that I should care?

Flow

  1. Start with the four headline numbers. Total Revenue, Orders, AOV, Basket. Boring but necessary, and the rest of the report sits on top of them.
  2. Every one of them gets a YoY delta stamped underneath. A number without motion is a photograph, not a story.
  3. The daily line is noisy. Read the 7-day moving average instead, that's the actual trajectory.
  4. Then the concentration question. If the top five pizzas own most of the revenue, every downstream recommendation has to respect that.

Graphs

  • exec_kpi_*KPI rowThe four headline numbers with a YoY delta on each one.
  • exec_trendLine chartDaily revenue (noisy) with a 7-day moving average overlaid in green. Read the green line.
  • exec_donut_catDonutRevenue share by category. Biggest slice first.
  • exec_top5Bar + shareTop 5 pizzas and the share of revenue they own.

DAX

Total Revenue
SUMX(Data_pizza, Data_pizza[quantity] * Data_pizza[price])
Revenue YoY %
VAR _C = [Total Revenue]
VAR _P = CALCULATE([Total Revenue], SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR(Dim_Date[Date]))
RETURN DIVIDE(_C - _P, _P)
Revenue 7D Moving Avg
VAR _R = DATESINPERIOD(Dim_Date[Date], MAX(Dim_Date[Date]), -7, DAY)
RETURN DIVIDE(CALCULATE([Total Revenue], _R), 7)
Top 5 Revenue Share
VAR _T5 = TOPN(5, VALUES(Data_pizza[name]), [Total Revenue], DESC)
RETURN DIVIDE(CALCULATE([Total Revenue], _T5),
    CALCULATE([Total Revenue], ALL(Data_pizza[name])))

Gaps

Why the business is growing isn't on this page. Could be more orders, heavier baskets, a price hike, or a good quarter for a specific category. The next three pages take that apart.
NextWhether demand is consistent or spiky goes on page 2. Volume vs price is a page 3 question.

P2Demand Rhythm

Question

When does the money come in, and is the rhythm stable enough to staff against it?

Flow

  1. The heatmap is the whole page. A year of hourly orders, flattened into one picture.
  2. The dark-red blocks are peaks. The hourly and weekday bars underneath are sanity checks in case one giant Friday is dragging an average around.
  3. MTD pacing is the only forward-looking thing on the page. If it's red, this month is running below the same day last month, and someone needs to know before month-end rather than at it.

Graphs

  • dem_heatWeekday by hour heatmapRevenue intensity per slot. The redder the cell, the busier that hour of that weekday.
  • dem_hourlyColumnRevenue by hour of day, all weekdays pooled.
  • dem_dowBarRevenue by weekday.
  • dem_ident_*KPI tilesPeak hour, weekend vs weekday, MTD pacing.

DAX

Peak Hour Revenue
MAXX(VALUES(Dim_Time[Order Hour]), [Total Revenue])
Weekend Revenue
CALCULATE([Total Revenue], Dim_Date[WeekdayNo] IN {6,7})
Revenue MTD Prior
VAR _D = DAY(MAX(Dim_Date[Date]))
VAR _PE = EOMONTH(MAX(Dim_Date[Date]), -1)
VAR _PC = DATE(YEAR(_PE), MONTH(_PE), MIN(_D, DAY(_PE)))
RETURN CALCULATE([Total Revenue],
    DATESBETWEEN(Dim_Date[Date], DATE(YEAR(_PE), MONTH(_PE), 1), _PC))
Revenue Pacing %
DIVIDE([Revenue MTD] - [Revenue MTD Prior], [Revenue MTD Prior])
Cell vs Weekday Avg % (tooltip)
VAR _C = [Total Revenue]
VAR _A = CALCULATE(AVERAGEX(VALUES(Dim_Time[Order Hour]), [Total Revenue]),
    ALL(Dim_Time[Order Hour]))
RETURN DIVIDE(_C - _A, _A)

Gaps

Why a slot is busy, the heatmap can't tell you. One catering order of forty pizzas looks identical to forty one-pizza orders. Basket shape lives on page 4.
External drivers like weather, events, marketing, payday aren't in the file. A Friday spike is just a Friday spike here.

P3Menu Performance

Question

Is the revenue coming from a few heroes, or spread across a long tail?

Flow

  1. Put every SKU on one chart. Price on the X, quantity on the Y. That's the entire menu in one picture, which is the whole reason this visual earns the biggest real estate on the page.
  2. Median reference lines slice the chart into four zones. A blob of dots becomes four decision buckets.
  3. The Pearson coefficient next to the chart is the sanity check. Strongly negative means the classic price-elastic story holds. Near zero means something else is driving it, and I'd go look.
  4. The category and size bars are cross-checks, not headlines. Glance, confirm, move on.

Graphs

  • sell_scatterQuadrant scatterPrice vs quantity per SKU with median reference lines. Quadrants are the decision frame.
  • sell_cat_revBarRevenue by category, sorted down.
  • sell_size_barBarRevenue by size.

DAX

Median Pizza Price
MEDIANX(VALUES(Data_pizza[name]), CALCULATE([Avg Pizza Price]))
Median Pizza Volume
MEDIANX(VALUES(Data_pizza[name]), CALCULATE([Total Quantity]))
Correlation Coefficient
VAR _T = ADDCOLUMNS(VALUES(Data_pizza[category]),
    "@X", CALCULATE([Avg Pizza Price]),
    "@Y", CALCULATE([Total Quantity]))
VAR _AX = AVERAGEX(_T, [@X])
VAR _AY = AVERAGEX(_T, [@Y])
VAR _N  = SUMX(_T, ([@X] - _AX) * ([@Y] - _AY))
VAR _DX = SQRT(SUMX(_T, ([@X] - _AX) ^ 2))
VAR _DY = SQRT(SUMX(_T, ([@Y] - _AY) ^ 2))
RETURN DIVIDE(_N, _DX * _DY)

Gaps

Margin is unknown, so the ranking is revenue. A premium low-volume pizza might quietly be the highest-margin product in the menu, and this page will make it look like a dud. Keep that caveat with you.
Standalone vs basket is hidden at SKU level. A pizza sold alone and one that only sells as an add-on look identical on this chart. Page 4 is where that gets checked.

P4Order Behaviour

Question

How do customers actually order, and does the basket data change anything I thought about the menu?

Flow

  1. Stop thinking about pizzas for a second and go one level down. Ingredients are the supply-side reality. If Mozzarella disappears from the supplier on a Tuesday, half the menu disappears with it.
  2. The ingredient scatter plots quantity and revenue reach across every recipe. Top right is the list of things I really don't want to run out of on a Friday night.
  3. Then the basket shape. Multi-Item vs Single-Item, and the distribution of order sizes. If multi-item dominates, the lever is bundles and upsells. If it doesn't, the lever is per-SKU quality and price.

Graphs

  • ins_ingredientScatterEach ingredient plotted by how much volume and revenue it touches across the menu.
  • ins_ingredient_statsPivotTable (FillRule)Ingredient table. Background colour is bound to the metric, hot rows are above median.
  • ins_ordertypeColumnSingle-Item vs Multi-Item orders, side by side.
  • ins_basket_sizeHistogramOrder sizes. Read the mode and read the tail. A fat tail means there's a catering segment hiding.

DAX

Multi-Item Order %
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT(Data_pizza[order_id]),
        Data_pizza[Order Type] = "Multi-Item"),
    [Total Orders])
Avg Basket Size
DIVIDE([Total Quantity], [Total Orders])
Ingredient Revenue (bridge)
CALCULATE(SUM(Data_pizza[Revenue]),
    FILTER(Data_pizza, Data_pizza[pizza_id] IN
        SELECTCOLUMNS(
            FILTER('Ingredients Normalised',
                'Ingredients Normalised'[Ingredient] =
                SELECTEDVALUE('Ingredients Normalised'[Ingredient])),
            "pizza_id", 'Ingredients Normalised'[pizza_id])))

Gaps

No customer ID. So I can't say 'this person comes back every Friday'. There's no cohort, no retention rate, no repeat-order number. Just orders.
Anything that happens across visits is invisible. The ceiling for basket analysis is one order, and I had to take the engineering round to swallow that.

P5Action & Decisions

Question

What do I actually do next week, and how will I know it worked?

Flow

  1. Rank every SKU by revenue and slap the month-over-month rank change right next to it. Climbers and fallers stop hiding in the table.
  2. Three buckets come out of it. Protect is the top decile, the list that should never run out of stock and never get repriced without a test. Test-retire is the bottom decile on revenue and volume together. Promote is the premium but quiet corner from page 3.
  3. HHI sits on the page as a concentration gauge. A big Protect list with a high HHI is also a fragility list, and that's a different read.
  4. If a recommendation doesn't have a metric that moves when it works, it's a wish rather than a test. I have a rule about that.

Graphs

  • perf_topRanked listTop 10 SKUs. This is the Protect list.
  • perf_botRanked listBottom 10 SKUs. This is the Test-retire candidate list.
  • perf_rankRank tableAll SKUs with share, AOV, and rank-delta columns.
  • perf_hhiKPI tileCategory concentration (HHI). Close to 1 means fragile, near 1/N means spread out.

DAX

Rank Pizza by Revenue
RANKX(ALL(Data_pizza[name]), [Total Revenue], , DESC, Dense)
SKU Rank Delta
VAR _C = [Rank Pizza by Revenue]
VAR _P = RANKX(ALL(Data_pizza[name]),
    CALCULATE([Total Revenue], DATEADD(Dim_Date[Date], -1, MONTH)),
    , DESC, Dense)
RETURN _P - _C
SKU Share of Revenue
DIVIDE([Total Revenue],
    CALCULATE([Total Revenue], ALL(Data_pizza[name])))
Category Concentration HHI
VAR _S = ADDCOLUMNS(VALUES(Data_pizza[category]),
    "@s", DIVIDE(CALCULATE([Total Revenue]),
        CALCULATE([Total Revenue], ALL(Data_pizza[category]))))
RETURN SUMX(_S, [@s] * [@s])
SKU Best Pair (tooltip)
VAR _C = SELECTEDVALUE(Data_pizza[name])
VAR _O = CALCULATETABLE(VALUES(Data_pizza[order_id]),
    Data_pizza[name] = _C)
VAR _P = CALCULATETABLE(
    SUMMARIZE(Data_pizza, Data_pizza[name],
        "_c", DISTINCTCOUNT(Data_pizza[order_id])),
    TREATAS(_O, Data_pizza[order_id]),
    Data_pizza[name] <> _C)
RETURN CONCATENATEX(TOPN(1, _P, [_c], DESC), Data_pizza[name])

Gaps

No cost data. Every recommendation on this page is a revenue-impact estimate, not a profit one. Any Retire decision should be checked against cost before it becomes permanent, and I would not skip that step.

Data model

A light star schema. One fact table, a handful of dimensions, two bridges, and a calculated table that exists only to hold measures.

  • Data_pizza. The fact table. One row per order line.
  • Dim_Date and Dim_Time. Time intelligence doesn't work without these.
  • dim_pizza, dim_size, Pizza Category. The three lookups.
  • Ingredients Normalised. Bridge table. Without it, page 4 isn't possible.
  • Pizza Pair Counts. Co-occurrence counts for the basket-pair work.
  • measurements. Empty calculated table. Every DAX measure in the model lives here, grouped into six folders.

Data cleaning

All of it sits in Power Query M. The move that actually matters is the ingredient explosion. Ingredients come in as a comma-separated string in the Excel file. I split on the comma, unpivot, trim every value, then take distinct.

#"Split"     = Table.SplitColumn(..., Splitter.SplitTextByDelimiter(",", QuoteStyle.Csv), ...)
#"Unpivot"   = Table.UnpivotOtherColumns(..., {"pizza_id"}, "Attribute", "Value")
#"Trim"      = Table.TransformColumns(..., {{"Value", Text.Trim, type text}})
#"Distinct"  = Table.Distinct(...)

Trim before distinct, not after. If it runs the other way, "Mozzarella" and "Mozzarella " (with a trailing space) count as two different ingredients and the whole page 4 analysis gets corrupted. Found that one the hard way.

Lessons learned

  • tableEx ignores FillRule. I spent a while fighting this before giving up and switching to pivotTable for any row-coloured table.
  • Scatter reference lines need xAxisReferenceLine and y1AxisReferenceLine, specifically. The generic referenceLine property quietly double-renders.
  • Tooltip pages are a Desktop-only flag. Don't write pageInformation.type in page.json. Validate will pass, Desktop will refuse to open the file.
  • Theme bleed is real. barChart.dataPoint.defaultColor silently overrides Series colours. Chased that one for about an hour.
  • Validate isn't render. pbi report validate will happily green-light things Desktop rejects on load. The only real test is Desktop actually opening the file.
